## Tier Evaluation

**EU Commission Draft Guidelines on High-Risk AI Systems (May 25):**

The European Commission published draft guidelines clarifying which AI systems qualify as "high-risk" under the EU AI Act — the classification that triggers the Act's most burdensome obligations (conformity assessments, risk management systems, data governance requirements, human oversight, post-market monitoring).

**Why this matters for LSPs:**
- Operationalizes the EU AI Act's most consequential threshold. Until now, high-risk classification has been a source of compliance ambiguity for legal AI vendors and corporate legal departments
- Directly relevant to the standing view's AI Governance Doctrine theme and the multi-state/multi-jurisdiction governance fracture pattern
- Affects scope of advisory work for global corporate clients deploying AI in HR, credit, employment, education — categories where legal AI tools (contract review for employment, e-discovery touching personal data) may sit adjacent
- However: these are **draft** guidelines (consultation phase), not final; and they clarify rather than expand the existing Act

**Assessment:**
- Novelty: 3 (operationalizes existing framework; not new law)
- Materiality: 3 (clarifies compliance scope; consultation-stage)
- Specificity: 4 (concrete classification criteria)
- This is closer to Tier 2 than Tier 1 — useful for the EU compliance watchlist but not "must know before first meeting"

**Disposition:** Tier 2. Include in ledger with brief inclusion = No. Flag for watchlist update.
