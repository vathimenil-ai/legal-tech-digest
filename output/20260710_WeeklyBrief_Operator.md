## What Changed

**1. Relativity promotes its product chief to president and repositions as a "legal data intelligence" platform**

- **What happened:** Relativity named CPO Chris Brown as president to drive an AI "legal data intelligence" platform and integrate Gavel, its recently acquired document-automation and contract-review tool.
- **What's new:** Until now, the Relativity dependency conversation centered on where matters get hosted and reviewed, and on the pricing pressure of cloud migration. Elevating the product leader to the presidency and explicitly rebranding around "legal data intelligence" signals something different: the platform intends to climb into the analytics, automation, and orchestration tier — not just host the data underneath it. Folding contract review and automation into the core extends Relativity's reach into workflows that sit adjacent to managed services.
- **Why it matters:** For LSPs whose differentiation lives in the layer *above* hosting and review — analytics, workflow design, governed delivery — this is scope encroachment, not just cost pressure. The defensible position narrows to what a platform vendor structurally cannot own: human-accountable delivery, provable governance across models and jurisdictions, and independence from any single platform's roadmap. An LSP whose value proposition is "we orchestrate on top of Relativity" now has a platform partner with declared ambitions in that same space.
- **Competitive read:** Relativity is now both the platform LSPs sit on and a declared competitor for the intelligence layer above it — the partner-versus-dependency tension moves from pricing to product scope. [Sources: 1, 2]

**2. New survey puts a hard number on the discovery governance gap**

- **What happened:** A 1H 2026 industry survey found 69% of organizations deploying LLMs in discovery, but only 57% documenting how, with roughly 30% running production AI on inconsistent processes.
- **What's new:** Prior adoption-gap evidence was framed at the level of the profession broadly. This narrows it to discovery deployers specifically and shifts the frame from "value not realized" to "process not documented" — a defensibility problem, not just an ROI problem. Undocumented AI process is the exact failure mode that becomes a liability the moment a matter is challenged.
- **Why it matters:** This is a procurement-usable statistic. Discovery service providers that can demonstrate documented, auditable, repeatable AI process can point to a concrete number — one in three deployers can't — and price the difference as risk reduction, not as a feature. It converts governance from a soft selling point into a named liability the buyer already owns. [Sources: 3]

## Market Implications

**Margin and pricing** [opportunity sharpening]: The governance-gap survey hands discovery providers a specific lever for defending price. When one in three organizations is running production AI on undocumented processes, the pitch stops being "our AI is better" and becomes "our process is defensible and yours may not be." That reframes managed delivery as risk insurance, which is far harder to commoditize than review throughput. The threat side is equally concrete this week: Relativity's move up-stack means the platform itself may begin capturing the analytics and automation margin that has been available to providers building on top of it. [Sources: 1, 3]

**Platform and ecosystem** [risk shifting]: The Relativity dependency story changed character this week. The concern has been migration economics and the 2028 Server sunset — cost and timing questions. Brown's promotion and the "legal data intelligence" repositioning add a scope question: a platform with public-market-grade growth ambitions that expands into contract review, automation, and analytics is monetizing layers that were previously partner territory. LSPs whose platform dependency is concentrated in a single vendor now face not just pricing exposure but roadmap exposure — the vendor's growth path may run directly through the differentiating work its channel partners perform. This strengthens the case for platform independence as a strategic hedge, not merely an operational preference. [Sources: 1, 2]

**Workflow orchestration** [strengthening as requirement]: The governance survey is the important signal here. It moves auditable orchestration from a differentiator toward a defensibility floor. With 30% of deployers running production AI on inconsistent processes, the market is telling us that undocumented workflow is the norm, not the exception — which means the ability to *prove* how an AI reached a result is scarce and valuable. To satisfy what a challenged matter now demands, an LSP's orchestration layer must demonstrably include: documented model and version provenance, per-step process records, access controls, and human sign-off that survives scrutiny. On the competitive side, Relativity's up-stack move and Exterro's shipped agentic forensic tool confirm the orchestration tier is now contested by platform vendors and forensic vendors alike — connectivity and features no longer distinguish anyone. What distinguishes is provable governance and human accountability that a software vendor cannot structurally deliver. [Sources: 1, 3]

**Recurring revenue** [continuation]: The path from episodic discovery to embedded operations runs through exactly the gap the survey quantifies. Helping clients document, standardize, and defend their AI processes is not a one-time project — it is an ongoing governance-and-enablement relationship. Providers positioning here can turn the deployment-to-defensibility gap into a recurring advisory and managed-process line. This continues the pattern established in prior weeks around governance migrating toward a services and enablement layer rather than a standalone tool. [Sources: 3]

**Competitive position** [tension sharpening]: The week draws a sharp line. On one side, a platform vendor (Relativity) and a forensic vendor (Exterro) are both climbing into orchestration and automation. On the other, the durable LSP position is what neither can replicate: model-agnostic, jurisdiction-aware, human-accountable delivery backed by provable process. The governance survey suggests demand for that position is real and largely unmet. But the Relativity move is a warning that the technology layer is being absorbed from multiple directions at once — differentiation built on tools or integration breadth will keep eroding, while differentiation built on defensibility and accountability holds. [Sources: 1, 2, 3]
