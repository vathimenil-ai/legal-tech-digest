# Legal Tech Intelligence — Monday, June 22, 2026
*Thomson Reuters moves twice: a flagship rebuild reframed around agents, and survey data admitting the industry isn't yet getting value from AI.*

## Bottom Line

- The single most usable item today is Thomson Reuters' own survey finding that most professionals now use AI at work but far fewer get value from it — and that the shortfall is costing firms clients, talent, and the development of junior staff. The dominant legal-AI vendor has effectively published the business case for managed delivery: the gap between deploying AI and extracting value from it is exactly the gap a governed, orchestrated service model exists to close.
- Thomson Reuters also opened early access to a rebuilt CoCounsel Legal, and the substance is in the repositioning, not the refresh. TR is recasting its flagship assistant as an agentic system that plans and executes multi-step legal work, not a tool that waits for prompts. The contested value layer is moving from "answer a question" to "run the workflow" — the same ground managed-review and orchestration operators occupy.

## What Changed

**1. Thomson Reuters reframes CoCounsel Legal as an agentic legal "operating system."**
- **What happened:** TR opened early access to what it calls the next generation of CoCounsel Legal, its biggest rework of the assistant since the 2023 Casetext acquisition.
- **What's new:** The repositioning matters more than the rebuild. TR is no longer describing a research-and-drafting assistant. 
The next generation of CoCounsel Legal is described as a unified agentic platform that plans, selects tools, retrieves authoritative content, and adapts mid-workflow just as a senior associate would, not a first-year waiting for the next instruction.
 TR has separately stated its objective is 
to build an extensible operating system for legal work, one that allows new tools, workflows, and models to evolve on top of it.

- **Why it matters:** The competitive surface is climbing from the question-answer layer to the workflow-execution layer — the territory orchestration and managed-delivery operators have treated as their own. An incumbent that owns both the legal content and an agentic execution layer narrows the space above which an LSP must demonstrate added value. The defensible position is the part TR's assistant does not provide: model-agnostic, auditable orchestration across multiple vendors and jurisdictions, with a record of which model touched which data. This is early access and beta, not general availability, so the timing and pricing of a full release will determine how fast the bar actually rises. [Sources: 1, 4]

**2. Thomson Reuters' "Future of Professionals" survey puts costs on the AI adoption-value gap.**
- **What happened:** TR's new research finds most professionals use AI at work but far fewer derive value, and names the consequences: lost clients, departing talent, and slower development of junior professionals.
- **What's new:** This converts a familiar talking point into dual-sourced, vendor-neutral data with named business costs. 
Artificial Lawyer characterizes current AI adoption as slow and chaotic.
 Until now the "adoption is ahead of value" theme was largely anecdotal; it now carries a price tag from the industry's largest legal-AI vendor.
- **Why it matters:** Firms that have bought AI and aren't realizing returns are the precise buyer a governed delivery model converts. The pitch shifts from "we have AI" to "we close the distance between the AI you bought and the outcomes you expected" — a statistic that supports a managed-delivery sales motion is now available from a neutral source. For product positioning, the implication is to anchor on realized outcomes rather than feature breadth or adoption metrics. [Sources: 2, 3]

## Watch Next

- Whether TR sets general-availability timing and pricing for the rebuilt CoCounsel Legal, and whether its "operating system for legal work" framing is matched by integrations that let it run end-to-end matters — the difference between a better assistant and a genuine workflow displacer.
- Whether the survey's named costs — client and talent loss — start appearing in buyer RFPs as explicit value-realization or outcome requirements, which would convert the adoption-value gap from a marketing line into a procurement criterion.
