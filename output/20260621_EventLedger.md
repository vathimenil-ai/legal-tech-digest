## Tier Evaluation

### EU Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) — Sovereignty Tiers Become a Concrete Compliance Architecture

Days after the G7 sovereign-AI alarm (prior ledger Entry 1), the EU's proposed **Cloud and AI Development Act establishes a four-level assurance framework for cloud sovereignty** as part of the broader Tech Sovereignty Package. What was a head-of-state *concern* on June 18 now has a *regulatory instrument* attached: graduated sovereignty tiers that cloud and AI providers — and by extension the legal-tech and managed-services platforms built on them — will be classified against. For multinationals (especially EU-exposed clients), this converts "jurisdiction-resilient AI" from a procurement preference into a tiered compliance variable.

**Materiality: 4. Tier 1.** This is the regulatory complement to the sovereign-AI continuity thesis: the same week heads of state worried aloud about American-model dependence, the EU published the mechanism that operationalizes sovereign cloud/AI assurance levels. For an LSP, this strengthens the case that model-agnostic, jurisdiction-aware orchestration is a buyable compliance requirement, and it gives a concrete framework to cite in cross-border AI governance advisory. (Note: the proposal itself is dated June 3; it qualifies as a daily item because it is surfacing now in law-firm guidance and because it materially advances the live sovereign-AI thread — but flag the date in any client-facing use.)

### GSA Proposes LLM Data-Safeguarding Clause for Government Contractors

The GSA proposed a revised GSAR clause (552.239-7001) imposing **substantial obligations on contractors processing government data through LLMs**. This is a direct, federal-procurement-level requirement on how AI systems handle government data — relevant to any LSP or legal-tech provider serving federal clients.

**Materiality: 3. Tier 2.** A concrete new procurement-layer AI obligation that flows into the AI governance doctrine and the "AI-ready data governance" advisory thesis (H4). Adds a federal-contracting dimension to the data-handling compliance surface.

### Estonia Proposes Official Digital IDs for AI Agents

Estonia's government backed a plan to issue **"AI ID codes" — state-recognized digital identities for AI agents** that would carry permissions and accountability. This is the first national-level answer to agent identity, authorization scope, and liability.

**Materiality: 3. Tier 2.** Directly addresses the Autonomous Agent Governance Gap (Emerging theme). As agent-to-agent architectures spread (cf. prior Ironclad/Legora item), state-recognized agent identity becomes a governance primitive worth tracking for downstream procurement and liability allocation.

That is the regulatory complement to our continuity thesis — and a citable basis for selling jurisdiction-aware, model-agnostic orchestration into EU-exposed multinationals. **Action for President/GM:** fold CADA tiers into the cross-border continuity narrative. **Action for Head of Product:** treat the four-tier structure as a potential EU-deployment roadmap input. *Caveat: proposal dated June 3, single-source — verify before client-facing use.*

2. **Federal procurement is tightening LLM data handling (Tier 2).** The GSA's proposed clause is another compliance-spec source for government-facing AI work — feeds the AI-ready data governance advisory line.

3. **Estonia is defining agent identity (Tier 2).** As agent-to-agent architectures spread in legal tech, national agent-ID regimes become a governance and liability primitive to monitor.

**Net:** No new theme — but the sovereign-AI risk thesis, which went diplomatic earlier this week, now has a concrete EU regulatory instrument attached. The continuity/sovereignty story is maturing from concern → diplomacy → regulation in a single week.
