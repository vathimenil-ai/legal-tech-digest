# Legal Tech Intelligence — Monday, May 4, 2026

## Bottom Line

- **The revenue shift from review to collection now has a number.** Industry estimates project that document review's share of eDiscovery spend will fall from 62% to 52% by 2030, while collection rises from 17% to 25%. For legal services providers built around review economics, this is a five-year countdown to rebalance toward upstream data services.

## What Changed

### eDiscovery revenue composition forecast: review down 10 points, collection up 8 points by 2030

**What happened:** ComplexDiscovery published reconciled market estimates projecting that document review — the single largest revenue line for most discovery providers — will lose 10 percentage points of total eDiscovery task spend by 2030. Collection's share rises nearly proportionally, from 17% to 25%.

**What's new:** The directional claim that AI will compress review revenue has been widely assumed. What's new is a specific, quantified forecast of the composition shift. The 62%-to-52% decline in review share, paired with the 17%-to-25% rise in collection, reframes the challenge: this is not just margin compression on existing work, but a structural redistribution of where eDiscovery dollars flow.

**Why it matters:** Most large discovery providers derive the majority of their revenue from review-stage work — attorney review, quality control, privilege logging. A 10-point share decline over five years means that even if the total eDiscovery market grows, review-heavy operators will see their core revenue line shrink relative to the market. The growth is moving upstream, toward collection and data acquisition — work that requires different capabilities, different staffing models, and different technology investments. Legal services firms already investing in information governance and data handling at the collection stage are better positioned for this shift. Those whose AI investments are concentrated on making review faster may be optimizing a shrinking share of the pie.

**Important caveat:** These are reconciled estimates from a single industry publication without disclosed methodology. They should be treated as directional planning input, not precision forecasts. No second source provides comparable figures.
