## Tier Evaluation

### Anthropic $65B Series H at $965B Post-Money Valuation

Anthropic closed Series H on **May 28** at **$965B post-money** — exceeding the previously reported $900B figure tracked on the watchlist. Round led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia. Anthropic is now positioned as a near-trillion-dollar enterprise AI vendor with explicit legal-vertical product (Claude for Legal, launched May 12-14) and the largest enterprise customer base by share. This closes the watchlist item "Anthropic $50B/$900B round closure" and confirms capital asymmetry at the level previously hypothesized.

**Materiality: 4. Tier 1.** Resolves a watchlist item, confirms the capital backdrop behind the disintermediation thesis (H5), and changes how any LSP should price the durability of "frontier vendor moves up the stack" — Anthropic now has the balance sheet to sustain Claude for Legal investment indefinitely regardless of near-term legal-vertical revenue.

### Florida Supreme Court — Statewide Mandatory Rule on AI Hallucinations

The Florida Supreme Court issued an order today (May 28) creating a new rule applicable to **all Florida state courts** requiring the signer of any filed document to represent that "the legal authorities identified exist and are accurately cited." This is the **first state-supreme-court-level mandatory rule** addressing AI hallucinations across an entire state judicial system, contrasted with NY Part 161 (court-system rule) and prior local/federal sanctions. The rule applies immediately to all civil, criminal, family, and appellate filings.

**Materiality: 4. Tier 1.** This is materially different from prior sanctions cases because it is a **statewide affirmative attestation rule**, not a post-hoc sanctions tool. It creates a per-filing compliance obligation that touches every law firm and litigation support provider operating in Florida. Combined with NY Part 161 (effective June 1) and the EDPA sanctions case, this establishes the AI attestation regime as a multi-jurisdiction reality.

### Kirkland & Ellis $500M Custom AI Investment

Kirkland & Ellis announced a **$500M, 3-4 year investment** in custom AI tools and services built in-house. This is the largest disclosed AI capex commitment by any law firm and signals a strategic pivot: the world's highest-grossing law firm is choosing to build rather than buy from foundation model vendors or legal tech platforms. Notably reported alongside Claude for Legal's launch and the broader trend of in-house AI development by elite firms.

**Materiality: 4. Tier 1.** Strategic implications for LSPs: (1) the largest law firm clients may increasingly insource workflows that LSPs traditionally provided; (2) procurement preferences shift toward custom-built / firm-controlled solutions, complicating LSP product distribution to elite firms; (3) signals that at the top of the market, AI capability is being treated as a core competence rather than a buy-from-vendor function. Affects H3 (revenue compression), H5 (disintermediation vectors expand to include client-side build).

### Miller v. Regions Bank — Career-Altering Sanctions for ChatGPT Account Deletion

Alabama District Judge Mooty imposed **public reprimand, disqualification, licensing referral, and six-month suspension from practice** on counsel who intentionally deleted his ChatGPT account to destroy evidence of AI use. This is the most severe AI-misconduct sanction reported to date — it combines AI misuse with spoliation, and the spoliation-of-AI-records frame is novel.

**Materiality: 3. Tier 2.** Reinforces the AI records-as-evidence theme (Fortis v. Krafton lineage) and adds spoliation as a sanctioning frame. Strengthens demand for AI interaction preservation policies as governance service.

### Conservation Law Foundation v. Shell — Court Orders Production of Expert AI Prompts

Magistrate Judge Farrish ordered plaintiff to produce the **prompts an expert used in preparing her report**, treating them as part of the expert's methodology. This is one of the clearest rulings to date establishing AI prompts as discoverable in expert testimony contexts.

**Materiality: 3. Tier 2.** Creates a concrete discovery-design question for any LSP supporting expert work: prompt logging, retention, and production protocols must now be part of expert engagement workflows. Reinforces the broader pattern of AI-use auditability becoming a litigation deliverable.

### Analyst Note for President/GM and Head of Product

**Three Tier 1 developments today:**

1. **Anthropic's $965B Series H** removes the "capital constraint" defense in the disintermediation thesis. Claude for Legal will be funded through any IPO-timing uncertainty. The strategic implication is that the defensible LSP position must be established assuming Anthropic invests in the legal vertical indefinitely.

2. **Florida Supreme Court statewide AI attestation rule** combines with NY Part 161 (effective Monday, June 1) to make AI use attestation a multi-jurisdiction reality. Verify within the litigation services product line that attestation-supporting features are in place for both FL and NY filings by month-end.

3. **Kirkland & Ellis $500M custom AI build** is the most strategically provocative signal of the week. At the top of the market, AI is being treated as a build-not-buy core competence. This argues for sharpening LSP offerings against the middle market (AmLaw 100-500 and corporate legal departments) where build-it-yourself economics do not apply — and asking whether the LSP itself should disclose comparable AI capex to position with elite firm clients.

**Two Tier 2 developments** worth flagging:
- **Miller v. Regions Bank** — first six-month practice suspension for AI-related spoliation; escalates the severity ceiling on AI misconduct.
- **Conservation Law Foundation v. Shell** — first clear ruling that AI prompts in expert work are discoverable methodology; product feature requirement for any LSP supporting expert engagements.
