**Legal Tech Intelligence — Saturday, June 27, 2026**
*A second frontier vendor enters legal — and names its integration partners*

## Bottom Line

- Perplexity has launched a legal product built on multi-model routing and integrations with tools like Clio and Carta. The news isn't that another AI vendor entered legal. It's that frontier vendors are now competing directly at the workflow-orchestration layer — the same connective tissue that managed-services providers rely on to differentiate. Tool connectivity alone is no longer a moat.
- For the first time, a frontier vendor has named the legal-tech tools its offering plugs into. The partners Perplexity chose differ from the ones Anthropic chose. That tells legal services firms which parts of the stack are being absorbed into frontier platforms — and which remain open ground.

## What Changed

**Perplexity launches "Computer for Counsel," its first legal product, weeks after Anthropic's legal push.**

- **What happened:** Perplexity unveiled Computer for Counsel on June 24, a legal configuration of its agentic "Computer" platform, available to its Enterprise and Max subscribers.

- **What's new:** Two things sharpen this beyond "another AI vendor in legal." First, it is explicitly model-agnostic. 
Computer for Counsel adds model routing, MCP connectors, and a review step on top, and the lawyer still checks each cited claim before it ships.
 That is an orchestration play, not a chatbot. Second, and more useful, the integration partners are now named. 
Perplexity currently integrates with LegalZoom, Deel, Clio, DeepJudge and Carta, while Anthropic currently integrates with Definely, e-discovery providers Consilio, Everlaw and Relativity, and document management providers.
 The two frontier vendors picked different corners of the stack. Anthropic went toward e-discovery and document management; Perplexity went toward contracting, entity management, and practice operations.

- **Why it matters:** The positioning is deliberately not a head-on attack on research incumbents. 
Perplexity is not trying to out-Westlaw Westlaw; it targets the work before, around, and after formal research.
 For managed-review and discovery operators, that is the more important signal than the launch itself. Frontier vendors are not yet contesting the regulated, defensible core of discovery work — but they are wiring themselves into the surrounding firm tools that feed it. The analyst covering this market was blunt about the limit of the technical edge: 
multi-model routing is no longer unique, since Harvey routes across vendors too, and the real differentiator is reach into the open web and everyday firm tools.
 The durable advantage for legal services providers narrows further — away from "we connect to everything" and toward provable governance, jurisdiction-awareness, and human-accountable delivery on top of whatever model is routed underneath. That a frontier vendor now embeds Relativity (via Anthropic) on one side and Carta on the other also reframes platform dependency: the tools legal services firms build on are themselves becoming endpoints inside frontier-vendor orchestration. ## Watch Next

- Whether OpenAI or Google ships a comparable legal-vertical product in the coming weeks. Two frontier launches in a month reads as a cadence; a third would confirm it as the structural shape of the market rather than two coincidences.
- Whether enterprise buyers begin asking frontier vendors and their integration partners for access-control and verification commitments before adopting. One trade outlet already framed the launch as a prompt for firms to review AI access controls and client-confidentiality safeguards. 
Perplexity's Computer for Counsel should prompt firms and legal departments using Docusign, NetDocuments, DeepJudge or Carta-connected workflows to review AI access controls, verification protocols and client confidentiality safeguards before agentic tools become embedded in daily legal work.
 If that hardens into procurement language, governed-delivery operators gain a concrete selling point.
