## Tier Evaluation

### Claude for Legal Has Over 90 AI Agents

Artificial Lawyer reports today that Claude for Legal — launched May 12-14 with 12 main plugins and 20+ MCP connectors as the publicly disclosed scope — in fact ships with **over 90 AI agents** behind the plugin surface. This is a roughly 7-8x expansion of the agent count that was visible to the market when the launch was first analyzed. The prior ledger and Standing View treated Claude for Legal as a comprehensive but bounded vertical product. The 90-agent disclosure repositions it as an agent factory targeting the full breadth of legal workflows — not a curated set of practice-area products.

**Materiality: 4. Tier 1.** This is materially new information about a Core-theme development. The disintermediation surface is substantially larger than previously priced. For Head of Product: every LSP service category should now be evaluated against the question "does Claude for Legal already have an agent for this?" — the answer is almost certainly yes for far more categories than the May 12-14 disclosure suggested. For the President/GM: the Frontier AI Disintermediation theme moves from "comprehensive vertical product" to "agent-scale vertical product," which compresses the timeline on which LSP differentiation by workflow coverage remains viable.

### Kirkland & Ellis — Fine-Tuning Open-Source LLMs + Innovation Hiring Binge

Follow-on to the May 28 announcement of K&E's $500M, 3-4 year AI build. Today's reporting (Artificial Lawyer) discloses two material new facts: (1) the build strategy involves **fine-tuning open-source LLMs to create K&E's own legal AI model**, not just orchestrating commercial APIs; and (2) K&E is on a hiring binge for innovation roles to staff the program. This shifts the K&E story from "large capex commitment" to "specific architectural choice with talent strategy attached."

**Materiality: 3. Tier 2.** Materially new since yesterday's ledger. The fine-tuning approach is strategically significant: it means K&E is building a proprietary model asset, not just an orchestration layer. This raises the strategic stakes for LSPs because (1) a fine-tuned firm-proprietary model is harder for vendors to displace than a commercial-API-orchestrated workflow, and (2) other AmLaw firms with comparable budgets now have a credible build playbook to copy. Reinforces the "client-side build" disintermediation pathway added to H5 yesterday.
