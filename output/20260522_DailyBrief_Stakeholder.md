## What Changed

### 1. AI-generated work product is now a production eDiscovery data source

**What happened:** Relativity announced it can collect Claude Enterprise data into RelativityOne via Anthropic's Claude Compliance API.

**What's new:** This is the first direct integration between the dominant eDiscovery platform and a frontier AI vendor. Until now, AI interaction records were a theoretical discovery concern debated in law review articles and CLE panels. Relativity building a shipping collection pipeline makes it concrete: Claude interactions are ESI, and they flow into the same platform where every other category of corporate data gets reviewed and produced.

**Why it matters:** Three immediate consequences for legal services providers. First, a new service line: collecting, reviewing, and producing AI interaction records requires new workflows, new custodian identification protocols, and new review playbooks — none of which exist at scale today. LSPs that build these capabilities first capture early-mover advantage in a data category that will only grow as Claude Enterprise adoption scales. Second, governance advisory demand intensifies: corporate legal departments must now treat what employees type into Claude the same way they treat email — with preservation obligations, litigation hold protocols, and records management policies. Managed services firms with strong governance practices can turn this into recurring engagement. Third, Relativity's move is strategically revealing. Rather than competing with Claude, Relativity is treating Claude output as raw material for its platform — expanding its data scope and reinforcing its centrality in the eDiscovery ecosystem. That makes Relativity *more* entrenched, not less, in a world where AI-generated content proliferates. [Sources: 1]

### 2. Harvey targets the in-house legal buyer who procures LSP managed services

**What happened:** On Day 2 of the Harvey Forum, Harvey announced Contract Intelligence — a contract review product designed for in-house legal teams, currently accepting waitlist signups.

**What's new:** Harvey's prior products served law firms. Contract Intelligence is aimed at corporate legal departments — the same buyers who hire LSPs and ALSPs for managed contract review, due diligence, and compliance review. In two days, Harvey has assembled a coherent enterprise legal AI platform: Command Center for governance and adoption management, DeepJudge for institutional knowledge integration, and now Contract Intelligence for the actual contract work. This is the most complete legal AI product suite on the market.

**Why it matters:** Contract review is one of the highest-volume, most commoditizable managed services revenue streams. If Contract Intelligence delivers acceptable quality for routine contract work, in-house teams have a path to reduce their reliance on outsourced managed review. The waitlist status creates a window — likely three to six months before production clients — but the direction is clear: Harvey is competing for the GC's budget, not just the partner's workflow. LSPs and ALSPs whose contract review positioning depends on labor arbitrage rather than governance, domain expertise, or cross-workflow integration face the most direct threat. Providers whose value proposition includes end-to-end auditability across matter types have more defensible ground, but that ground is narrowing. [Sources: 2, 3]
