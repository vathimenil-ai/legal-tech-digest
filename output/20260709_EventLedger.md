## Tier Evaluation

### Illinois AI Safety Measures Act (SB 315) — Fourth State Enacts Comprehensive AI Governance

Governor Pritzker signed SB 315 into law July 6, described as **"the most rigorous safety standard"** among U.S. states, imposing multiple transparency requirements on AI developers and deployers. This is a genuinely new, dated primary development that moves the Standing View's state count from three enacted comprehensive regimes (CA, NY, CT) to four, and — importantly — raises the ceiling on stringency. For an LSP, each new comprehensive state regime widens the multi-jurisdiction compliance surface that feeds governance-advisory and readiness demand (H4), and reinforces the state-vs-federal-preemption tension. **Materiality 4. Tier 1.**

### UKJT Legal Statement on Liability for AI Harms

The UK Jurisdiction Taskforce published a Legal Statement finding that **existing English law already governs liability for AI harms**, including that professionals may be liable for AI-assisted work. This is an authoritative (industry-led, LawtechUK-convened) framing document that removes a "we need new law first" excuse and effectively confirms that human-accountable delivery is the liability locus today. This directly reinforces the Standing View's narrowed orchestration perimeter (human-accountable, auditable delivery) and the agentic-governance tension. **Materiality 4. Tier 2** (analysis-adjacent but a primary authoritative statement, single jurisdiction).

### US v. Heppner — Public GenAI Communications Not Privileged

A federal court held that a defendant's communications with a public generative-AI platform were **not protected by attorney-client privilege or work product**, in a health-care investigation context. This is a concrete court ruling on the AI-privilege tension already tracked in the Standing View, and it lands squarely on the investigation-readiness / AI-ready data governance thesis (H4): it creates a discipline requirement (don't route privileged/compliance matters through public tools) that LSP-governed, provenance-aware delivery can satisfy. **Materiality 4. Tier 2** (single ruling, reported via law-firm alert).

### Analyst Note for President/GM and Head of Product

**Three developments today, one Tier 1, two Tier 2 — all on the AI-governance / accountability axis, and mutually reinforcing.**

1. **Illinois SB 315 (Tier 1, materiality 4).** A fourth state now has comprehensive AI governance, and it's the most stringent to date. The multi-front enforcement surface — described as "unchanged" in the current Standing View — moved this week. The recurring theme holds: each new regime widens the readiness-advisory opportunity and hardens the case for a repeatable multi-state AI-compliance offering. **Action:** update the state count (3→4) and flag "most rigorous" as the new design floor for jurisdiction-aware controls.

2. **UKJT liability statement (Tier 2, materiality 4).** Authoritative confirmation that professionals are *already* liable for AI-assisted work under existing law — no legislative grace period. This is the strongest near-term argument for making documented human oversight a first-class, logged feature and for positioning governed delivery as liability management.

3. **Heppner privilege ruling (Tier 2, materiality 4).** A concrete court holding that public GenAI communications can forfeit privilege. Converts an abstract tension into a client-facing risk and an opening for privilege-preserving, governed workflows over shadow public-tool use.

**Net:** A rare day where the primary signal is squarely legal-vertical and directionally consistent — regulators (IL), an authoritative taskforce (UK), and a court (Heppner) all pushing accountability onto the professional/deployer. This tightens, rather than changes, the Standing View's narrowed orchestration perimeter: **auditable, jurisdiction-aware, human-accountable, privilege-preserving delivery.** The Harvey/Syntheia token-growth items (Tier 2/3, discarded from the table) quietly corroborate the adoption-acceleration backdrop against which this governance surface is expanding.
