### Tier 1 Detailed Evaluation

**1. Colorado SB 189 Signed May 14 — Detailed Legal Analyses Now Available Reveal Material Shift in AI Governance Framework**

Multiple major law firm analyses published May 18-19 (Baker Botts, Jackson Lewis, Alston & Bird, JD Supra roundup) provide the first detailed breakdowns of Colorado SB 189, signed by Governor Polis on May 14. The standing view captured the Colorado "repeal and replace" as a known development, but classified it at a high level as "transparency-only framework replacing mandatory bias audits." Today's detailed analyses reveal the shift is more nuanced and materially different from what was previously assessed:

**New material information from today's analyses:**
- SB 189 shifts accountability from **system-level** to **individual decision-level** — employers are now accountable for individual consequential decisions made using AI, not for the AI system's overall risk classification (Jackson Lewis)
- The new law eliminates the concept of "algorithmic discrimination" entirely and replaces it with a **notice- and disclosure-based regime focused on automated decision-making** (Alston & Bird)
- The **small business carve-out is eliminated** — the original law exempted small-business deployers; the replacement applies to all deployers regardless of size (Alston & Bird)
- The law creates new pathways for **private AI litigation** — while removing mandatory audits, it opens new private causes of action (Alston & Bird: "Creating New Trails for AI Rules and Private AI Litigation")
- The effective date replaces SB 205 "mere weeks before" the original law's June 30, 2026 effective date — confirming the regulatory whiplash for companies that had been preparing for SB 205 compliance

**Why this is materially new for the standing view:**
The prior assessment was that Colorado was "deregulating" — moving from mandatory audits to lighter-touch transparency. Today's analyses reveal Colorado is actually **restructuring**, not deregulating: shifting from system-level governance (which LSPs could provide as a service) to individual-decision-level accountability (which requires different compliance infrastructure), while simultaneously creating new private litigation pathways. This changes the governance service demand calculus: the service isn't "conduct bias audits" but "build decision-level audit trails and prepare for private litigation."

**Assessment:** The development itself (SB 189 signing) was known. But the material specifics now available fundamentally change how the standing view should characterize Colorado's shift. This is a **standing view amendment** rather than a genuinely new development. **Tier 2** — important for standing view accuracy but not a "before your first meeting" signal.
