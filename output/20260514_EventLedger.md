### Tier 1 Detailed Evaluation

**1. Carta Acquires UK ALSP Avantia, Launches "Carta Law" — AI-First Law Firm Embedded in ERP Platform**

Two independent sources confirm that Carta, the private markets ERP platform valued at over $8.5 billion, has acquired Avantia, a UK-based Alternative Business Structure (ABS) law firm founded by Slaughter and May alumnus James Sutton. Avantia handled managed legal services for asset managers. The combined entity launches as "Carta Law," integrated directly into Carta's ERP platform.

**Why this is material at Tier 1 for LSP executives:**

1. **This is structurally different from prior AI-native law firm signals.** Manifest OS ($60M) and Moritz/Parlai ($9M) are venture-backed startups building AI-first law firms from scratch. Carta Law represents a **technology platform acquiring an existing law firm and embedding it into enterprise software infrastructure.** This is a platform-to-legal-services pathway — not a legal-services-enhanced-by-AI pathway.

2. **Carta is not a legal tech company.** It is a financial technology ERP platform used by thousands of private market participants for cap table management, fund administration, and equity management. Its acquisition of a law firm means **a non-legal enterprise platform is vertically integrating legal services into its workflow.** This is a direct insourcing/disintermediation vector: Carta's customers no longer need to engage external law firms or ALSPs for the managed legal services Avantia provided — the services are now embedded in their existing software.

3. **The UK ABS structure is the enabling mechanism.** This acquisition is possible because UK regulation allows non-lawyer ownership of law firms through Alternative Business Structures. This is the exact regulatory arbitrage the standing view should track — technology platforms acquiring law firms in jurisdictions that permit non-lawyer ownership, then potentially extending those capabilities to clients globally.

4. **This is the third AI-native/AI-first law firm data point in recent weeks** (after Manifest OS and Moritz/Parlai), but it represents a qualitatively different model: platform acquisition rather than venture creation. The AI-native law firm pattern is not just "startups building AI law firms" — it now includes "technology platforms acquiring law firms."

5. **Direct implications for ALSP/LSP market.** Avantia was an ALSP serving asset managers. Its acquisition by Carta removes it from the independent ALSP market and converts its services into a platform feature. If other technology platforms follow this model (e.g., enterprise software companies acquiring ALSPs to embed legal services), the addressable market for independent ALSPs and LSPs narrows.

**Materiality: 4** — A technology platform acquiring a law firm and embedding legal services into enterprise software is a structural development that changes how LSP executives think about competitive threats. This is not another AI tool — it is vertical integration of legal services into non-legal platforms.

While Claude for Legal was covered as the Tier 1 item in the May 13 ledger, today's feeds provide **materially new quantitative detail** that was not available yesterday:

The editorial frames this as a market structure re-segmentation, noting "any procurement officer, contract manager, eDiscovery director, privacy counsel, or IP partner running a vendor evaluation this quarter is doing so against a new market structure."
- **TechCrunch** reports Ramp expense data showing **Anthropic now has more business customers than OpenAI** — 34.4% of surveyed businesses paying for Anthropic vs. 32.3% for OpenAI. This is the first data point showing Anthropic surpassing OpenAI in enterprise adoption.

**Why this update is material beyond yesterday's entry:**

The 20+ MCP connectors number is significantly larger than the 2-3 integrations (Thomson Reuters, Free Law Project, Everlaw) identified yesterday. This means Claude for Legal is not just connecting to a few legal data sources — it is building a **broad interoperability layer** across the legal software stack. The 12 practice-area plugins confirm this is a comprehensive vertical product, not a narrow launch.

Anthropic surpassing OpenAI in business customer count provides **demand-side context** for the Claude for Legal launch: Anthropic is now the most widely adopted AI provider among businesses (by this measure), making Claude for Legal's market reach potentially larger than a CoCounsel/OpenAI-dependent competitor.

**Assessment:** This adds materially to the May 13 Tier 1 entry but does not warrant a standalone new Tier 1 entry. Include as a **Tier 2 update** to avoid duplicating the prior ledger while ensuring the new data is captured.
