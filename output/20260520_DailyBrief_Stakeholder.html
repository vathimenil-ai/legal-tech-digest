Notable signals below the action threshold:

Three developments today reinforce existing patterns without crossing the threshold of "read this before your first meeting":

  • Andreessen Horowitz led a $10.5M seed round into Stilta, a five-month-old Swedish startup building agentic AI for patent invalidity and infringement analysis. This is the fourth distinct AI-native legal services model to attract serious venture capital in the past two months (after Manifest OS at $60M, Moritz/Parlai at $9M, and Carta Law's ALSP acquisition). The a16z signal matters more than the startup itself: institutional venture capital is now betting that AI agents can replace human analysis in patent litigation — one of the highest-billing, most specialized practice areas in law. If that thesis holds, the pattern extends to any practice area built on structured analytical tasks.

  • OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy joined Anthropic's pre-training team. This is the highest-profile talent move between the two leading frontier AI companies and arrives weeks after Claude for Legal launched. It reinforces the talent concentration at Anthropic at a moment when the company is actively building out its legal vertical.

  • Eudia announced a partnership with OpenAI to co-build AI for US government legal and acquisition teams. This is the first confirmed instance of OpenAI partnering with a legal AI vendor for deployment to a named government client's legal function. The government legal market operates differently from commercial legal services, but this confirms that OpenAI is actively moving from model provision into sector-specific deployment — the pattern previously tracked only as speculation.
