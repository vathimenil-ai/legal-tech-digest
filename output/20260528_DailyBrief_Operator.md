*Financial regulators move first on frontier AI — and a New York court rule lands in four days.*

## Bottom Line

- New York's financial regulator has issued the first formal US guidance directing banks and insurers to manage frontier AI cybersecurity risk. Most of the largest US financial institutions — and many of the legal services industry's biggest customers — now have a fresh, named compliance obligation to assess against by quarter end.
- A New York court rule governing attorney AI use in court submissions takes effect Monday, June 1. Litigation support providers serving NY matters need attestation and audit-trail workflows live within four days, not as a future product feature.

## What Changed

**NYDFS issues twin Industry Letters on frontier AI cybersecurity and heightened cyber threat environment.**

- **What happened:** 
The New York State Department of Financial Services issued two Industry Letters on May 21: an Advisory on heightened cybersecurity risks associated with frontier AI models, and companion guidance on measures regulated entities should consider in a heightened cybersecurity threat environment.

- **What's new:** This is the first time a major US state financial regulator has issued formal supervisory guidance specifically aimed at frontier AI risk inside the institutions it regulates. NYDFS set the template the rest of the country copied after its original Part 500 cybersecurity rule, so the practical question is no longer whether other state and federal banking regulators follow, but how fast. The guidance also pulls the "upstream AI model provider risk" question out of the abstract — frontier model choice is now a supervised variable for banks and insurers, not just a procurement preference.
- **Why it matters:** NYDFS-regulated institutions are among the highest-spending buyers of investigation readiness, regulatory response, document production, and compliance advisory work. A named regulatory expectation issued seven days ago creates an immediate assessment-and-gap-analysis surface across that entire customer base. Legal services providers that can package a frontier AI readiness assessment tied directly to the Advisory's scope — and do it before parallel guidance arrives from the OCC, Federal Reserve, and FDIC — capture the first wave of advisory mandates. The same guidance also sharpens supply chain compliance as a procurement criterion: regulated buyers now have a documented reason to scrutinize which foundation models sit underneath any AI tool they purchase, which favors providers with clean, auditable supply chains.
