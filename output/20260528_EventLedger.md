## Tier Evaluation

### NYDFS Frontier AI Advisory + Cyber Threat Guidance (May 21, 2026)

The NY Department of Financial Services issued **two Industry Letters** to regulated entities: one specifically on "Heightened Cybersecurity Risks Associated with Frontier AI Models" and a companion "Guidance on Measures Regulated Entities Should Consider in a Heightened Cybersecurity Threat Environment." This is the **first major U.S. financial regulator** to issue formal guidance specifically targeting frontier AI cybersecurity risk for its regulated population.

This matters for three reasons: (1) NYDFS is the most influential US state financial regulator and a precedent-setter (the original cybersecurity regulation Part 500 was widely copied); (2) the guidance creates concrete compliance obligations for the largest concentration of US financial institutions, which are major LSP customers for governance, investigation, and document production; (3) it converts the "frontier AI as compliance variable" theme (currently Emerging) into hard, surfaced regulatory expectation. The standing view's watchlist item "NY DFS rulemaking proceedings" was Elevated specifically anticipating this kind of issuance.

**Materiality: 4.** This is Tier 1 — it sets a benchmark other state financial regulators (and potentially federal agencies) will reference, and it creates immediate advisory demand among NYDFS-regulated entities.

### Illinois Proposed Rules on AI in Employment (May 15, 2026)

IDHR issued Notice of Proposed Amendments under Public Act 103-804 governing AI in employment. This is the rulemaking layer beneath an already-enacted state law. Adds another state to the multi-state AI employment governance patchwork (CO, CA, NY, CT, now IL operationalizing).

**Materiality: 3.** **Tier 2.** Confirms multi-state fracture; adds compliance-orchestration surface.

### NY Part 161 — Court Rule on AI in Litigation (Effective June 1, 2026)

New York court rule requiring disclosure/governance of AI use in papers submitted to NY state courts, effective June 1. This is a **procedural court rule**, not legislation — and it directly affects discovery, deposition prep, and pleading workflows where LSPs operate. Combined with the EDPA sanctions case and prior AI-hallucination jurisprudence, this is the first major-jurisdiction court rule operationalizing AI disclosure. Materially relevant to litigation support service design (audit trails, attestation workflows).

**Materiality: 3-4.** **Tier 2.** Worth ledger entry — creates concrete service-design obligations for litigation support providers in NY effective in 4 days.

### DOJ Antitrust on Algorithmic Pricing as Criminal Risk (May 14, 2026)

Acting Deputy AAG Daniel Glad signaled DOJ Antitrust Division view that certain AI/algorithmic pricing activities may constitute criminal antitrust violations. This is a **prosecutorial position statement**, not a charging decision. Strengthens the investigation-readiness theme but at a pattern level rather than operational level.

**Materiality: 3.** **Tier 2** — flag in ledger for H4 reinforcement.
